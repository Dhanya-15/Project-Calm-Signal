The good and the bad

Information gathered about the pros and cons of AI and technology in digital mental health.

Positive

  • Easy access - Help is on your phone, even in remote areas.
  • Low cost - Many apps are free or cheaper than regular therapy.
  • Always available - Support for 24/7, not just office hours.
  • Less stigma - People can start privately and at their own pace.
  • Early detection - Tracking can show problems before they grow.

Negative

  • Privacy risk - Very personal data can be leaked or sold.
  • No human touch - A bot cannot truly understand or care like Human.
  • Wrong advice - AI can make mistakes or sound too sure.
  • Emergencies - Tools may unexpectedly fail to respond to a real crisis.
  • Unequal access - Not everyone has a phone, internet or privacy at home.

Three policies for safer digital mental health

Policy 1

Protect data and ask consent

Mental health data is deeply personal, so users must stay in control of it.

  • Collect only the data the service needs.
  • Encrypt it and never sell it.
  • Let users see, download and delete their data.
Policy 2

Keep a human safety net

AI can support people, but a real person must be there when it matters most.

  • If AI detects a crisis, show helplines and connect to a person.
  • AI supports care but does not diagnose.
  • Licensed professionals review the advice the AI gives.
Policy 3

Be honest and fair

People should know what they are talking to, and everyone should get a fair chance to use it.

  • Always tell users when they are talking to a bot.
  • Test the AI for bias and fix it regularly.
  • Offer free, low-data and multi-language options.
Summary

Technology that helps without harm

Technology can make mental health support easier to reach. With strong privacy, a human safety net and honest, fair design, it can help people without putting them at risk.
